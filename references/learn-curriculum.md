# Learn Curriculum (module bank)

The curriculum builder in `curriculum-builder-prompt.md` selects and orders modules from this bank for each learner.

Four tracks, thirteen modules. Teach one module at a time, one concept per message. Each module lists the goal, the key points to land, the misconception to correct, and a check question. Adapt the wording and examples to the user's level.

## Contents
- Track A: Foundations (1-4)
- Track B: Understanding businesses (5-8)
- Track C: Markets and timing (9-11)
- Track D: Behavior and process (12-13)

---

## Track A: Foundations

### 1. What a stock is and why prices move
- **Goal:** Understand ownership, and that price = what buyers and sellers agree on today.
- **Key points:** A share is a slice of a business. Prices move on changing expectations about future earnings, interest rates, and sentiment. Short term is noisy; long term follows business results.
- **Misconception:** "A falling price means the company is worth less." Price and value can diverge for years.
- **Check:** If a company's profits double but its stock falls 10% in a month, name two reasons that could happen.

### 2. Stocks, ETFs, and index funds, and how to buy
- **Goal:** Know the building blocks and the basic mechanics.
- **Key points:** Individual stock versus a basket (ETF, index fund). Fees (expense ratios). Brokerage accounts, market versus limit orders, why limit orders protect you. Dividends versus growth.
- **Misconception:** "More holdings always means more diversification." Ten tech stocks are not diversified.
- **Check:** Why might a beginner own an index fund before picking individual stocks?

### 3. Risk, return, and diversification
- **Goal:** Understand that return comes from taking risk, and what drawdown feels like.
- **Key points:** Volatility, drawdown (a 50% fall needs a 100% gain to recover), correlation, why diversification reduces risk without removing it, risk tolerance versus risk capacity.
- **Misconception:** "Risk means the chance of losing everything." Mostly it is the size and length of declines you must sit through.
- **Check:** A stock falls 40%. What gain gets you back to even?

### 4. Compounding and time horizon
- **Goal:** See why time in the market and low costs matter most for long-term investors.
- **Key points:** Compounding with a worked example (rule of 72), cost of waiting, fees compounding against you, inflation, the value of consistency.
- **Misconception:** "I need to start with a lot of money." Time and consistency matter more than the starting amount.
- **Check:** Roughly how long does money take to double at 8% a year?

## Track B: Understanding businesses

### 5. The three financial statements
- **Goal:** Read an income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement at a basic level.
- **Key points:** Income statement = performance over a period. Balance sheet = snapshot of assets, liabilities, equity. Cash flow = where cash actually came from and went. Why profit is not the same as cash.
- **Misconception:** "Net income is the truth." Accounting profit can hide poor cash generation.
- **Check:** Why might a profitable company still run out of cash?

### 6. The balance sheet ratios the scan uses
- **Goal:** Understand each filter in `criteria.md`.
- **Key points:** Debt-to-equity, interest coverage, current ratio, free cash flow, share dilution. What each protects against. Why sectors need different thresholds (banks versus software).
- **Misconception:** "Zero debt is always best." Some debt is healthy; the issue is fragility.
- **Check:** Which ratio tells you whether earnings can cover interest payments?

### 7. Valuation basics
- **Goal:** Know that price paid affects return, and that cheap is not the same as good.
- **Key points:** P/E, P/S, free cash flow yield, comparing to history and peers, margin of safety, value traps, why great businesses can be bad investments at the wrong price.
- **Misconception:** "A low P/E means it is a bargain." It may reflect declining prospects.
- **Check:** Give one reason a stock with a low P/E could still be a poor buy.

### 8. Business quality and moats
- **Goal:** Recognize durable competitive advantages.
- **Key points:** Brand, switching costs, network effects, scale, patents. Signals in numbers: stable or rising margins, high return on invested capital, consistent growth.
- **Misconception:** "Fast growth means a good business." Growth without profitability or a moat can destroy value.
- **Check:** Name a moat and one number you could check to see if it is working.

## Track C: Markets and timing

### 9. Market cycles, sectors, and the macro backdrop
- **Goal:** Understand why sectors lead and lag, and what drives cycles.
- **Key points:** Interest rates, inflation, earnings cycles, sector rotation, defensive versus cyclical sectors. Why the sector and news pass comes first in `scan`.
- **Misconception:** "I can predict the next recession." Even professionals mostly cannot.
- **Check:** Why do rising interest rates often hurt high-growth stocks more than utilities?

### 10. Reading the quarterly chart: SMA 50, regime, pullbacks, bottoms
- **Goal:** Understand how the scan reads the 3-month chart.
- **Key points:** What a 3-month candle is. A 50-quarter SMA is about a 12.5-year average, so it is slow by design. Regime first (SMA direction plus where price closes), then the setup that fits: pullbacks in uptrends, confirmed bottoms in downtrends, patience otherwise. Why "extended" means wait. What the chart cannot tell you: why the move happened or whether it continues.
- **Moving averages and RSI:** A moving average smooths price into a trend line. When a stock in an uptrend dips to or through it, buyers have often stepped in, which is the "buy the dip" idea many videos teach on short charts (4-hour or 1-month views). The scan applies the same idea to the 3-month chart and adds the regime check first. RSI scores recent strength from 0 to 100: 30 or below is called oversold, 70 or above overbought. It is context for how stretched a move is, not a timing signal.
- **Misconception:** "A chart signal predicts the future," and "oversold means it must go up, overbought means it must drop." Signals describe the current balance of buyers and sellers. In strong trends RSI can stay overbought for a long time, and a stock in a downtrend can stay oversold while it keeps falling. That is why the scan checks the regime before it looks for a dip.
- **Check:** In a bearish regime, why does the scan wait instead of buying a stock that just crossed below its moving average?

### 11. Information diet: filings, earnings, and noise
- **Goal:** Separate signal from noise.
- **Key points:** 10-K (annual) and 10-Q (quarterly) filings, earnings calls, where to find them (SEC EDGAR), why headlines move prices more than fundamentals in the short run, how to read a headline skeptically.
- **Misconception:** "Breaking news means I must act now." Most news is already in the price.
- **Check:** Where would you look to verify a company's reported debt level?

## Track D: Behavior and process

### 12. Investor psychology
- **Goal:** Recognize the biases that cost investors the most.
- **Key points:** Loss aversion, FOMO, herd behavior, recency bias, overtrading, panic selling at lows, anchoring on purchase price. Pre-committing to rules to counter emotion.
- **Misconception:** "Smart people are immune." Process, not intelligence, protects you.
- **Check:** What rule could you set today to avoid panic selling in a 30% drop?

### 13. Building a process
- **Goal:** Turn knowledge into a repeatable routine.
- **Key points:** Watchlist, written thesis and invalidation for each holding, general ideas about position sizing and diversification, review cadence (quarterly fits this toolkit), account types and taxes in general terms, when to consult a licensed professional.
- **Misconception:** "More activity means better results." For long-term investors, less often is usually better.
- **Check:** Write a one-sentence thesis and one invalidation condition for any company you know.

## Teaching tips
- Mobile screens are small: aim for 6-8 sentences per message.
- Use round numbers in examples so the math is easy to follow.
- Praise correct reasoning specifically, not generically.
- If the user is overwhelmed, shrink the concept, do not skip it.
- In learn mode, never recommend specific securities. Examples illustrate concepts only.
